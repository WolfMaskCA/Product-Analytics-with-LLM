Your trial converts at —. Is that good?

View 1 is what a standard analytics tool tells you: a funnel, a trend, a number. Accurate, and it stops there — the tool has no idea what good looks like, what to change, or whether the change worked.

View 2 is the same events with the conversation built in: where you stand, what to change, and what it did.

The product

A subscription fitness app. Install, hit a paywall on first launch, start a 3-day free trial, convert to paid or cancel.

The metric

Trial-to-paid: of the people who start the free trial, how many become paying subscribers.

The question

Not "what is the number." Everyone has the number. The question is whether it is good, what would move it, and how you would know if it moved.

What this view answers

  • What the conversion rate is, and how it moved week to week.
  • Where in the funnel people are lost, and how many.
  • Whether anything broke recently.

What it cannot answer

  • Is that good? The number is only comparable to itself. There is no outside anchor anywhere in the tool, so nobody in the room can say whether this is a crisis or a win.
  • What should we change? A funnel shows you the leak, never the cause. The next step has to be argued somewhere else.
  • What exactly would a developer do on Monday? "Improve trial conversion" is not a ticket.
  • Did the change work? By the time the next chart is drawn, the context for why it was drawn lives in someone's memory or a deck nobody opens.
So what happens next

Somebody exports this to a slide, writes the interpretation by hand, and the analysis immediately starts drifting away from the data. The chart and the argument now live in two different places, and only one of them updates. That gap is the entire product below.

Recommendation

Why this is different from a chart plus a slide

Chart tool + written analysis

  • The number lives in one system and its meaning lives in another.
  • The interpretation is written once, at one moment, and starts aging immediately.
  • A developer reads a paragraph and infers the change.
  • Six weeks later nobody can reconstruct why the change was made.

This dashboard

  • The benchmark, the diagnosis, the proposal and the result sit on the same data.
  • Every panel re-derives on filter, so the argument updates when the data does.
  • A developer reads the exact config key and the screen it affects.
  • The reasoning stays attached to the number, permanently.

Built as one self-contained file from a single atomic cube: cube[arm][week] = {installs, trials, paid, cancels, renewed}. Every panel here derives from that table, in Python at build time and again in JavaScript on every filter, so two panels cannot disagree. The generator re-derives each view twice and refuses to publish on a mismatch.
